Three edge controls for the group panel

All three keep the control on the group column itself, where the first mock's option B put it. They differ in what the control is, what "hidden" leaves behind, and whether the panel can come back on hover. Hover and click inside each window.

B1

Hover-reveal pin, auto-hide with peek

A small round pin sits faint in the bottom far corner of the group column and brightens while the mouse is over the column. Press it once and the column slides out of the way, leaving a 10 px edge strip. Hovering that strip peeks the panel over the canvas without moving anything; press the pin again while it is peeked and it sticks back in place.

Hover the group column to reveal the pin, then click it. In auto-hide mode, hover the edge strip to peek, and click the pin again to stick.

Auto-hidePanel is off-screen. A 10 px edge strip with a label and count marks it. Canvas gains 140 px.
PeekHovering the strip slides the panel over the canvas. Leaving it slides it back. The pin now sits tilted; pressing it pins again.

For

  • Nothing added to the layout at rest: the pin is invisible until you need it.
  • Groups stay one hover away, so hiding them costs almost nothing in workflow.
  • Pin / unpin is a pattern people know from Visual Studio, JetBrains and Windows taskbars.

Against

  • A control that hides itself is the hardest kind to find the first time.
  • Hover peeks need care: a mouse crossing the right edge on the way to the scrollbar would flash the panel. Needs a 150 to 250 ms hover delay.
  • Three states instead of two; the tilted pin is the only clue which one you are in.
  • Peek overlay does not work on touch; a click on the strip must also open it.
B2

Tab on the divider

The divider between the layer list and the group column becomes a real, draggable splitter handle, with a small tab at mid-height carrying a chevron. Click the tab to collapse or expand; drag the divider to make the group column narrower or wider; double-click it to toggle. Collapsed, the divider and tab stay at the window edge as the way back.

Click the tab in the middle of the divider, or double-click the divider itself.

For

  • Always visible, always in the same place; nothing to discover by accident.
  • Two states only, and the chevron always points the way the column will move.
  • Unlocks the splitter the code already has: dragging gives a wider group tree for long names, which nothing offers today.
  • The tab is what Finder, Explorer and most IDE sidebars use.

Against

  • Hidden state keeps a 6 px divider, so the canvas gains 134 px, not 140.
  • A draggable divider means the fixed-width arithmetic for compact screens has to learn about a variable group width.
  • The tab is a small target at mid-height, away from where the mouse usually is.
B3

Collapse to an icon rail

A chevron button sits at the bottom of the group column, always visible. Pressing it does not remove the column; it shrinks it to a 40 px rail: the Create Group button becomes a "+" tile, and each group becomes a small tile below it. Click a group tile and the column expands with that group selected. The rail stays useful while collapsed.

Click the chevron at the bottom of the group column. Then click a group tile in the rail to expand with that group selected.

For

  • Collapsed is still functional: Create Group and every group stay one click away.
  • The control is always visible, at the bottom where the mouse already goes for the layer buttons.
  • Two states, no hover behavior, works with touch and keyboard.

Against

  • Smallest gain: the rail keeps 40 px, so the canvas gains 100 px.
  • Group tiles need short labels; with many groups the rail needs its own scrolling.
  • More UI to build: tile rendering, selection sync with the tree, and a rail variant of the Create Group button.

Recommendation. B2 is the safest and cheapest: always visible, two states, and it turns the locked splitter into a real one. B1 is the most elegant once learned and gives the full 140 px, but the hover peek needs a delay and a click fallback, and first-time users have to find an invisible button. B3 is the pick if groups are used constantly and the goal is only to slim the panel, not to clear it. B1's pin can also be added on top of B2 later: the tab collapses, the pin decides whether collapsed means gone or peekable.
